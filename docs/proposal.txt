Modeling YouTube Video Engagement and Trending Popularity Jannagrace Carandang (924173494), Uzair Hamed Mohammed (920142896)
I. Description of the Problem
YouTube is the world’s largest video sharing platform, where creators and marketers strive to reach the coveted “Trending” tab.This project aims to explore the relationship between upload-time metadata factors, such as video category, publication hour, title length, tag count, comment/rating settings, and post-release video performance. Our objectives combine both prediction and inference: 1. Regression: Predict total logarithmic views and engagement ratios such as the ratio of likes to views or comment density.
2. Classification: Predict whether a video achieves “High Engagement” status, defined by top quartile views or exceptional engagement ratio.
3. Inference Goal: Determine whether creator-controlled decisions like publishing time, title length, comment settings, have statistically significant effects on audience reception.
II. Description of the Data The dataset, titled “Trending YouTube Video Statistics” (sourced from Kaggle, contributed by Mitchell Jolly), contains multiregional daily records of trending YouTube videos. We will focus primarily on the U.S. dataset (USvideos.csv), which consists of 40,949 observations across 16 variables .

day of week), title (derived into title word length and character count), tags (derived into tag count and presence of specific keywords), comments_disabled (binary), ratings_disabled (binary), and video_error_or_removed (binary).
● Response Variables 𝑌 : views, likes, dislikes, and comment_count.
III. Supervised Learning Framework This project utilizes Supervised Learning. We will benchmark at least three candidate statistical learning methods to predict video engagement metrics: Regularized Linear Regression (LASSO/Ridge) to predict continuous engagement metrics while handling high-dimensional categorical title/tag features, Tree-Based Methods (Random Forests/Gradient Boosting) to capture complex non-linear interactions like whether title length impacts views differently depending on the video category, and Logistic Regression & Support Vector Machines (SVM) for binary classification of high vs. low engagement tiers, utilizing different kernel functions (Linear vs. RBF). Model validation will be performed using 10-fold Cross Validation, and performance will be evaluated using Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) for regression and ROC-AUC/Error Rate for classification tasks.
IV. Potential Challenges & Concerns ● Severe Right-Skewness: Key quantitative metrics (views, likes, comment_count) exhibit heavy right-skewed distributions, requiring logarithmic transformations before model fitting.
● Data Leakage & Temporal Overlap: Videos appear on multiple days as metrics accumulate, so records must be deduplicated or grouped by video_id to prevent data leakage between training and testing sets.
● Missing Baseline Channel Metrics: The dataset lacks channel-level covariates (such as overall channel subscriber count), meaning baseline channel popularity may introduce unobserved heterogeneity.
